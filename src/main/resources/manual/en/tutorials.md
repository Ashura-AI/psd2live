@@ t-eye-blink | Learn "Eye Blinking" in 10 Minutes
This tutorial reaches the same goal as the Cubism beginner tutorial in PSD2Live: making the character blink. The difference is that PSD2Live generates blinking automatically from layer tags; your job is to prepare the artwork, check the result and reshape by hand where needed.
## Preparing the artwork
- Put the eye whites, irises and upper lashes on separate layers; layer names can be `eyewhite` / `眼白`, `irides` / `瞳孔`, `eyelash` / `睫毛`.
- Draw only the upper lashes on the lash layer and do not mix in the lower eyelid line, or closing the eye may pull the lower lid.
- Keep the left and right eyes separate, with the side in the name, such as `eyelash-l` or `左睫毛` (left and right are the character's own).
- If you need a specific closed-eye design, draw an extra `eye_close` / `闭眼` layer; otherwise the program generates the closed-eye curve from the lashes.
## Generating and checking the blink
1. Choose **File → New → New project from PSD…** ({key:OPEN_PSD}) and pick the PSD.
2. Keep **Default** under **Quick setup** on the start screen; after generation you enter the editing workspace.
3. Open the **Layers** panel and check that the eye layers' **Tags** show the right part and side; if not, open the tag and change it.
4. Open the **Parameters** panel and drag **Left Eye Open** and **Right Eye Open** (`ParamEyeLOpen` / `ParamEyeROpen`, 0 is closed, 1 is open) to check the closed-eye shape.
5. If the closed-eye curve or eye corner position is off, adjust **Blink sag**, **Blink corners** and **Closed lash thickness** under **Rig Values → Eyes** in the **Model presets**.
## Reshaping the closed eye by hand
1. Select the lashes or eye white in the Hierarchy and drag the parameter to the closed key (right-click a key mark to snap).
2. Switch to **Deform** mode ({key:MODE_DEFORM}) and reshape by dragging control points or with the brush ({key:TOOL_BRUSH}).
3. Once one side is done, use **Mirror keyform → Mirror to …** in the canvas context menu to write the shape to the matching object on the other side; the parameter is switched to that side's open/close parameter automatically.
4. Switch to **Preview** ({key:MODE_PREVIEW}) and turn on **Idle animation** to check the automatic blinking.
## Differences from the Cubism tutorial
- Cubism: create meshes by hand, add keys to the eye parameters, make each closed-eye shape, then copy it to the other side.
- PSD2Live: meshes, parameter keys and closed-eye shapes are generated from tags; manual editing is only for touch-ups, and edits are kept across regeneration.
- On export, a model with the Blink motion enabled gets an EyeBlink group in model3.json, so the runtime can blink automatically.

@@ t-mouth-aiueo | Learn "AIUEO" Mouth Movements in 20 Minutes
This Cubism tutorial combines the two parameters "mouth open/close" and "mouth form" into the five mouth shapes A, I, U, E, O (あいうえお). PSD2Live generates these two parameters and their keyforms for the mouth automatically at import; this tutorial checks the five mouth shapes on that basis and touches them up.
## Preparing the artwork
- Draw the mouth layer fully open with a clear outline, named for example `mouth` / `嘴巴`.
- The inside of the mouth can be drawn as one piece, or split into `tooth_t` (upper teeth), `tooth_b` (lower teeth) and `tongue`; these are automatically clipped to show only inside the mouth.
- If you care about the closed-mouth design, draw an extra `mouth_close` / `闭嘴` layer; otherwise the closed mouth is generated by the program.
## What the two parameters do
| Parameter | ID | Range | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mouth Open | `ParamMouthOpenY` | 0…1 | 0 closed, 1 fully open |
| Mouth Form | `ParamMouthForm` | -1…1 | Negative values turn the corners down and narrow; positive values turn them up and widen |
The five shapes are combinations of the two: "A" wide open, "I" slightly open and wide, "U" slightly open and narrow, "E" half open and a little wide, "O" half open and narrow.
## Checking and adjusting
1. After importing the PSD ({key:OPEN_PSD}), check in the **Layers** panel that the mouth, teeth and tongue **Tags** are correct.
2. In the **Parameters** panel, drag **Mouth Open** and **Mouth Form** and check the five shapes one by one using the combinations above.
3. Adjust the closed-mouth line in **Model presets → Rigging & Facial**: turn on **Generate lip outlines**, click **Mouth settings** to choose the **Initial mouth shape** (**Flat**, **Smile**, **W shape** or a custom curve), and set **Thickness** and **Sample edge color**.
4. Adjust the strength of the mouth forms in **Model presets → Rig Values → Mouth**, for example **Smile corners**, **Smile widen**, **Closed narrow** and **Lip seam**.
## Touching up mouth shapes by hand
1. Select the mouth ArtMesh in the Hierarchy and move **Mouth Open** and **Mouth Form** to the key to touch up (right-click a key mark to snap).
2. Switch to **Deform** mode ({key:MODE_DEFORM}) and adjust by dragging control points or with the brush {key:TOOL_BRUSH} and smooth {key:TOOL_SMOOTH}; turn on the brush's "Connected Only" to brush the upper and lower lips separately.
3. When you need more in-between shapes, click the parameter name to open **Parameter Properties**, click an empty spot on the **Key points** axis to add a point, then deform at that value.
4. Switch to **Preview** ({key:MODE_PREVIEW}) and drag both parameters continuously to confirm the in-between values transition naturally.
## Differences from the Cubism tutorial
- Cubism: add keys to mouth open/close and mouth form by hand, make the shape at each of the four corners, then check the five combinations.
- PSD2Live: the two parameters and their keyforms are generated from the artwork, the closed-mouth line and strength are adjusted with the model presets, and manual deformation is only for touch-ups.
- On export, `ParamMouthOpenY` is written to the LipSync group of model3.json, so the runtime can use it for lip sync directly.

@@ t-figure | Learn Live2D with Diagrams
This page explains what a Live2D model is made of through a few groups of concepts, and maps them to PSD2Live's UI names. Once these concepts are familiar, the automatically generated result is easy to read and change.
## ArtMeshes
Each PSD layer becomes an ArtMesh after import, the same as Cubism's ArtMesh: a texture plus the triangle mesh covering it. Moving mesh vertices deforms the texture with them. The mesh topology is changed in **Edit** mode and its shape in **Deform** mode.
## Deformers
A deformer is a "frame" that wraps ArtMeshes or other deformers. A **warp deformer** (Warp) bends its contents as a whole with a set of control points, and a **rotation deformer** turns its contents around a point. Deformers can be nested: a parent's motion passes to its children, for example the head turning carries the eyes and hair. Parent-child relations are shown in the **Hierarchy**; occlusion is decided separately by draw order.
## Parameters and keyforms
A parameter is a slider, such as **Angle X** or **Left Eye Open**. A key on a parameter records an object's shape at that value, which PSD2Live calls a keyform; when the slider is between two keys, the shape is interpolated automatically. Two parameters can form a 2D link and control the combined poses at the four corners together.
## Animation and physics
Animation changes parameter values over time; physics calculates sway from input parameters such as head and body and outputs it to parameters such as hair sway. Both only drive parameters and never change shapes directly.
## Differences from the Cubism tutorial
- The concepts are the same and the names match closely: ArtMesh, warp deformer, and the shape on a key, which PSD2Live calls a keyform.
- PSD2Live also has editing aids such as the skeleton and deform paths, which are baked into Cubism parameters, deformers and keyforms at export.
- The structure above is generated automatically when a PSD is imported, and can be inspected directly in the Hierarchy and Parameters panel.
Related pages: [[concept-of-artmesh]] · [[deformer]] · [[parameter]]

@@ t-psd | 1. Illustration Processing
This section corresponds to the first step of the Cubism basic tutorial: organizing the illustration into a layered PSD that can move. PSD2Live reads layer names to recognize parts, so layering and naming matter equally.
## File requirements
- A layered PSD in RGB, 8-bit, with a transparent background.
- Rasterize text, vectors, layer styles and adjustment effects, or merge them into the corresponding pixel layers first.
- Keep the body basically upright; the head may tilt slightly.
## Layering points
1. Separate front and back hair, and paint extra where they are hidden so nothing shows through when they move.
2. Put the eye whites, irises and upper lashes on separate layers; draw the irises complete, including the parts hidden by the eyelids.
3. Draw the mouth fully open; the inside of the mouth can be one piece or split into upper teeth, lower teeth and tongue. Draw an extra closed-mouth layer when you need a specific closed-mouth design.
4. Put left and right parts on separate layers with the side in the name; left and right are the character's own, so the character's left is usually on the right of the image.
5. Put body parts such as the neck, top, arms and legs on separate layers, keeping overlaps where they meet.
## Naming
Layer names that use part names in English, Chinese, Japanese or Korean are recognized automatically, for example `front hair`, `前发`, `eyelash-l`, `左睫毛`. Unrecognized layers are not dropped; after import you can add tags in the **Layers** panel to correct them. See [[precautions-for-psd-data]] for the full name table.
## Differences from the Cubism tutorial
- Cubism: split the artwork into parts; naming is mainly for your own organization.
- PSD2Live: layer names decide the generated parts and sides, so wrong names affect the generated result; tags can still be changed after import.
- Left and right parts drawn on the same layer can be split after import with **Model → Split Layers by Mesh…**; when one layer needs to pass through another part, use depth splitting.

1. Drag **Body Angle X**, **Body Angle Y**, **Body Angle Z**, **Body Lean** and **Breath** in the Parameters panel.

@@ t-motion-hint | Tips for Improving Motion Quality
This page collects general tips for more natural motion; these principles apply equally in PSD2Live.
## Tips
- **Set the timing before the details**: first arrange the timing of key poses in the **Dopesheet**, then adjust easing in the **Curves** view.
- **Ease in and out**: keyframes use Bezier easing by default; avoid constant speed where a motion starts and stops, and use the curve handles to slow the start and end.
- **Offset the timing**: do not start the head, body and gaze on the same frame; letting the body or gaze move slightly earlier or later gives the motion more depth.
- **Follow-through and overlap**: leave hair and accessories to physics instead of keying sway frame by frame; turn on physics in the preview to check.
- **Leave pauses**: blinking and small breathing keep still stretches from looking stiff; use the **Blink** and **Breath** controls of the idle motion.
- **Check the ends**: parameters held at the end of their range for long look stiff; widen the parameter range or adjust the physics output scale if needed.
*> During playback all canvases share the same pose, so you can adjust in the Animation Editor while watching the preview canvas.

@@ t-function | Introduction to Useful Features
This page introduces time-saving features in PSD2Live; see each manual page for details.
## Generation and regeneration
- **Automatic generation**: importing a PSD generates meshes, deformers, parameters, motions and physics from the model presets; see [[model-presets]].
- **Update Generated Rig**: **Model → Update Generated Rig** merges your edits onto a newer version of the generated result; see [[update-generated-rig]] and [[generated-overrides]].
- **Layout projects**: place loose artwork and generate, then add images and regenerate later; see [[layout-project]].
## Editing aids
- **Mirror keyform**: once one side is done, mirror it to the opposite key or the opposite-side object; see [[motion-mirroring]].
- **Skeleton**: pose with FK/IK, baked into parameters and keyforms at export; see [[skeleton]].
- **Glue and deform paths**: make neighbouring meshes move together, or bend a mesh along a path; see [[glue]] and [[deformpath]].
- **Swing generation**: generate swing shapes and physics for hair and accessories in one click; see [[auto-generation-of-sway-motion]].
- **Hair and clothing simulation**: simulate, then bake into parameters and pendulums; see [[simulation]].
## Artwork and textures
- **Split by mesh / depth split**: separate parts drawn on the same layer; see [[split-by-mesh]] and [[depth-split]].
- **Paint mode**: change layer pixels directly inside the project; see [[paint-mode]].
- **Texture upscale**: **Model → Texture Upscale…** ({key:TEXTURE_UPSCALE}); see [[texture-upscale]].
## Project management
- **Branching history**: history is kept as a tree, and you can switch to any version; see [[history-palette]].
- **Parameter snapshots**: save the current parameter combination in the Parameters panel and load it at any time.
- **Many export formats**: **File → Export as** exports runtimes, images and video; see [[other-formats]].
## Differences from the Cubism tutorial
- Cubism's introduction to useful features focuses on helper functions inside the Editor; PSD2Live's focus is automatic generation and keeping edits on top of the generated result.

@@ t-model-introduction | Try Making a Model Introduction Video
This section exports the model's motion as a video to showcase the model. Cubism needs a scene set up in the Animator before outputting video; PSD2Live exports motion video directly from the project.
## Preparing the motion
1. Create a new motion in the **Animation** panel (**New Motion → Blank Motion** or **Copy From Preset**).
2. In the **Animation Editor**, show the head turn, blinking, mouth shapes and body motion in turn: turn on **Auto Key** ({key:MOTION_TOGGLE_AUTO_KEY}), move the playhead and drag parameters to set keyframes.
3. Adjust easing in the **Curves** view, and check in the preview canvas with {key:MOTION_PLAY_PAUSE}.
## Exporting the video
1. Choose **File → Export as**, and under **Images and video** choose **MP4 video…**; for a transparent background choose **WebM video (with alpha)…** or **QuickTime ProRes 4444…**.
2. In the settings choose the **Motion**, and set **FPS**, **Size**, **Background** and **Physics** as needed.
3. Choose the output folder and export. Files are written to a folder named "<project name>-<format>" in the output folder.
!> Video formats are encoded with ffmpeg; MP4 has no transparency and is composited onto the background color.
## Differences from the Cubism tutorial
- Cubism: create a scene in the Animator, place the model and background, then output images / video.
- PSD2Live: there are no scenes or background tracks, and one motion is exported at a time; when you need a background, captions and so on, composite the transparent video in video editing software.
Related pages: [[exporting-images]] · [[other-formats]]

@@ interactive-beginner | Making My First Live2D Model
This track is for users making a Live2D model for the first time. It starts from layered PSDs, parts and parameters, then goes through import, editing, motion, physics and export in order, and does not require any Cubism experience.
## How the tutorials work
- The tutorials run inside the app and act on the real interface, not a demo screen.
- Each step highlights the control to use and switches to the relevant panel; shortcuts are shown with your current key bindings.
- Some steps continue automatically once you do the action; others continue when you click **Next** after reading. You can **Skip** or **Exit Tutorial** at any time.
- Except for "Basic workflow", each chapter first asks you to import a PSD or open a project when no model is open.
## Start learning
The chapters of this track are listed below; you can go through them in order from the first chapter, or click any chapter to start it on its own. You can reopen them any time from **Help** ({key:OPEN_HELP}).
